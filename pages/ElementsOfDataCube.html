Dimensions in different levels of hierarchies are measured by facts.

  1. Fact. The truth about something that happened. Mostly a number, and meaningless alone.
  2. Dimension. Labels give it meaning: when, where, what and who. This is relatively permanent master data.
  3. Hierarchy. Each dimension has levels, so you can look at the same sale from near or far.
  4. Measure. A calculation over the facts in view returns one scalar value.
  5. The punch line. All four words in one sentence.
Explain more

Fact

The truth about something that happened. Mostly numbers, recorded transaction by transaction. Meaningless without labels.

Dimension

Anything we want to measure using facts. Relatively permanent, with hardened edges. This is master data: mostly labels, and it gives facts their meaning.

Hierarchy

The different levels of a dimension, such as Year › Quarter › Month or Country › State › City.

Measure

Any calculated scalar value. A fact is stored; a measure is worked out for whatever is in view.
